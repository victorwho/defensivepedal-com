How do I start cycling to work if traffic scares me?

Start with the route, not the bike. Traffic is what holds most would-be cyclists back: for every person who rides, about six more would like to. A route on quieter streets is usually only a little longer (Defensive Pedal's routes average about 5% more than the shortest), so ride it on a weekend before your first workday.

Updated 27 November 2026 · Victor Rotariu · How the score works

Is it normal to be scared of cycling in traffic?

Yes, and you're in large company. For every person who cycles regularly there are roughly six who would like to and don't, and traffic is what stops most of them.

The fear is also pointing at something real. We tested our risk score against official crash records from five European countries. On the roads it rates worst, cyclists were about twice as likely to be seriously injured per journey as on the roads it rates best. The big, fast, busy roads that feel worst are the ones the data rates worst too.

What makes one street calmer than another?

Mostly speed and traffic. The streets our model rates highest-risk tend to have a limit of 50 km/h or more, several traffic lanes, heavy traffic and trucks. Those features predict how badly a crash ends rather than how often one happens, which is why they weigh so much.

The type of bike lane counts as well. A track separated from cars by a kerb or barrier gets the most credit. A painted lane helps on a 30 to 40 km/h street but counts for little beside fast traffic. A dashed advisory lane, which drivers are allowed to use, is scored as ordinary road.

How do I find a calmer route to work?

Look for a route that crosses the main roads instead of following them. Cities that score well usually have the quiet streets to make this work. Paris, for example, is 6th of 300 European cities for how much of daily life you can reach on connected calm streets.

A routing app built for this will find those streets for you. Defensive Pedal is one. In a test of 10,149 random trips in Romanian cities, its routes carried lower modelled risk than Google Maps' cycling directions on 99 trips out of 100, and 2.1 times lower on average. Google Maps is built to find the quickest route, so the comparison shows what each one is designed to do.

The cost is distance. Our routes average about 5% longer than the shortest, which adds roughly 250 metres to a 5 km commute.

How should I try the route the first time?

On a weekend, when the roads are quieter and nobody is waiting for you at work. Ride the whole route once at your own pace and note where you felt uneasy. If one stretch felt bad, look for a way round that stretch and keep the rest. Then ride it on a workday.

If even a weekend commute feels like a lot, start smaller: a trip to the shops or to a friend's place, on streets you already know.

What about junctions and roundabouts?

Our score can't help much here. It tracks risk along streets, but when we checked it against crash records it showed no signal at junctions, and junctions are where a lot of urban harm happens. So give the big ones your full attention. If a junction feels like too much, you can get off and walk across with the pedestrians.

What this guide doesn't tell you

  • Our risk score predicts how badly a crash ends, not whether one happens.
  • It shows no signal at junctions.
  • These are associations, not causes. Nothing yet shows that rerouting one rider changes that rider's own outcome.
  • Kit, bikes and bike-to-work schemes vary by country, and this guide leaves them out.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to cycle to work? It depends on the streets you use. On the roads our model rates worst, cyclists were about twice as likely to be seriously injured per journey as on the roads it rates best, so the route you pick matters more than anything else on this page.

Do I need a special bike to commute? This guide doesn't cover bikes or kit. It's about the route, which you can plan before you own a bike at all.

How much longer is a calmer route? About 5% longer than the shortest, on average, for Defensive Pedal's routes. On a 5 km commute that's roughly 250 metres.
