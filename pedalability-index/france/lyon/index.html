Is it safe to cycle in Lyon?

Lyon is one of Europe's lower-risk cities for cycling, by our measure. It ranks 26th of 300 in the Pedalability Index 2026, with a score of 85.7 out of 100 (higher means lower assessed risk), second in France after Paris. About 11% of its street length is rated High risk, almost twice Paris's 6%.

Lyon is a city of good islands. It ranks 10th in Europe for how many residents can reach daily life on calm streets, but 75th for a typical trip across town, because crossing between those islands still means using the big roads.

Why does Lyon rank where it does?

Each city in the index gets three scores: how risky its streets are (40% of the total), how risky a typical journey is (30%), and how much of daily life people can reach on connected calm streets (30%). Lyon's three results sit far apart.

Access is where Lyon is strongest. It's 10th in Europe: most residents can get to shops, schools and work without leaving low-stress streets.

Its streets overall rank 51st. A larger share of Lyon's street length is rated High risk than in Paris, Toulouse or Montpellier.

Journeys are the weak point, at 75th. We plan 210 everyday trips across each city and score the streets those routes use, and in Lyon a lot of cross-town trips end up on the main roads.

Put together, Lyon is calm where you live and harder when you cross town. Strasbourg has the opposite profile: 23rd for typical journeys, 138th for its streets.

Which streets in Lyon carry the most risk?

The riskiest main roads run along and across the rivers. Six of the 20 highest-risk are quais beside the Rhône, among them Quai Claude Bernard, Quai Jean Moulin and Quai du Docteur Gailleton. Most of the rest are big avenues and cours east of the Rhône: Avenue Tony Garnier in Gerland, which rates riskiest of all, Avenue Berthelot, Cours Gambetta, Rue Garibaldi and Cours Albert Thomas.

The bridges rate as high-risk too. Of the 16 named bridges over the Rhône and Saône, 13 rate as riskier than three in four of the city's main streets, and the Pont de Lattre de Tassigny, Pont Robert Schuman, Pont Gallieni and Pont Pasteur are among the highest-risk roads in Lyon. That's where the gaps in the network are. Many neighbourhoods are calm, but getting from one to the next often means a quai or a bridge.

Speed explains much of the pattern. Lyon has made 30 km/h the limit on almost all of its streets, keeping 50 km/h (70 in a few places) only on certain roads. The quais, avenues and bridges named above are mapped above 30 km/h for most of their length. Every lower-risk street named below is mapped at 30 km/h or less.

At the lower-risk end are main streets such as Rue Bataille, Cours Charlemagne, Avenue Général Frère and Cours Lafayette. Cours Lafayette shows how sudden the change can be: the street rates among the lowest-risk in Lyon, while the Pont Lafayette at its western end rates as riskier than three in four main streets.

These ratings describe how each street is built: its speed limit, traffic, number of lanes, heavy vehicles, and whether the bike lane is a separated track or a line of paint. If you ride in Lyon and think we've rated a street wrong, write to contact@defensivepedal.com. Corrections go back into the map.

Do Lyon's bike lanes count?

They do, and the type of lane matters. The model gives the most credit to a cycle track separated from traffic by a kerb or barrier; behind one, the speed of the road next to you stops counting against the street. A painted lane helps on a 30 to 40 km/h street, but beside traffic at 80 to 90 km/h most of that credit is gone. A dashed advisory lane, which drivers are allowed to use, is scored as ordinary shared road.

Lyon's main cycling programme is the Voies Lyonnaises, run by the Métropole: 13 cycle routes across 49 communes, planned to pass 350 km by 2030. The score reads lanes as they appear in OpenStreetMap when the model was last rebuilt, so a newly opened section counts once it is mapped and the model is rebuilt.

Is the score checked against real crashes?

Yes. We tested it against official crash records from five countries, France among them, covering 2,034 cyclist counting sites, about 4 billion cyclist journeys and 1,169 serious or fatal injuries. On the roads the score rates worst, cyclists were about twice as likely to be seriously injured per journey as on the roads it rates best. The study is public: Severity Without Frequency.

I'm new to cycling. Where do I start in Lyon?

Close to home. Lyon is 10th in Europe for access to calm streets, so your own neighbourhood is probably the easiest place to begin, with short local trips.

For a commute across town, plan the route before you buy the bike. The risk sits on the main connecting roads, so look for a route that crosses them instead of following them.

What this score doesn't tell you

The score predicts how badly a crash ends (injury severity), not whether a crash happens.

It was checked against official crash records from major European cities in five countries. That check found the score's signal along streets and not at junctions, where much urban cycling harm happens.

The figures are associations, not causes. Nothing yet shows that changing one rider's route changes their outcome.

A city's score is an average over its whole street network. Risk changes street by street.

Questions about cycling in Lyon

Is Lyon safer to cycle than Montpellier?

The two are too close to separate on this measure, because their likely rank ranges overlap. Lyon ranks 26th of 300 with a score of 85.7 (likely 13th–38th), and Montpellier ranks 31st with 83.6 (likely 27th–46th). Higher score = lower assessed risk.

What does Lyon's score of 85.7 mean?

It places Lyon 26th of the 300 European cities in the Pedalability Index 2026. The index runs from 0 to 100 on a fixed scale. Higher score = lower assessed risk. A score of 0.0 means the lowest of the 300 cities, not an absence of safety, and no score means a city is free of risk.

Does the score say how likely a crash is in Lyon?

No. The score predicts how badly a crash ends, not whether one happens. It also has no signal at junctions, where much urban cycling harm happens.

Is Lyon good for cycling?

On street risk, yes, compared with most of Europe: it's 26th of 300. It does best for local trips (10th for access to calm streets) and worse for trips across town (75th).

Is Lyon safer to cycle in than Paris?

Not by our measure. Paris ranks 13th and Lyon 26th. About 6% of Paris's street length is rated High risk, against about 11% of Lyon's.

Is Lyon better for cycling than Bordeaux or Marseille?

Yes, by a wide margin. Bordeaux ranks 128th and Marseille 222nd.
