Is it safe to cycle in Paris?

Paris is one of the lowest-risk large cities in Europe to cycle in, by our measure. It ranks 13th of 300 European cities in the Pedalability Index 2026, with a score of 93.7 out of 100 (higher means lower assessed risk). About 6% of its street length is rated High risk, against 16% in London.

Paris's riskiest main roads aren't in the centre: they form a ring. Almost all of the Boulevards des Maréchaux, the ring road just inside the Périphérique, scores among the highest-risk streets in the city.

Paris is the top French city in the index, ahead of Lyon (26th), Toulouse (33rd) and Strasbourg (38th). We re-ran the whole ranking 2,000 times under different assumptions, and Paris landed anywhere from 6th to 19th. Copenhagen and Barcelona move within much the same band, so the three are best read as one group.

What makes Paris score well?

Each city in the index gets three scores: how risky its streets are (40% of the total), how risky a typical journey is (30%), and how much of daily life people can reach on connected calm streets (30%).

Access is where Paris does best. It's 6th in Europe: most residents can get to shops, schools and work on a joined-up network of low-stress streets.

Its streets rank 17th. Most carry a 30 km/h limit, and in our model that limit accounts for more of Paris's score than anything else (more on that below, in "How did Paris get here?").

Typical journeys come 33rd. We plan 210 everyday trips across each city and score the streets those routes use, and in Paris some cross-town trips still have to use the big boulevards.

Which streets in Paris carry the most risk?

The riskiest main roads aren't in the centre. They form a ring. Almost all of the Boulevards des Maréchaux, the road that circles the city just inside the Périphérique, scores among the highest-risk streets in Paris: Boulevards Macdonald, Poniatowski, Masséna, Jourdan, Lefebvre, Davout, Mortier, Brune, Soult and Kellermann. The single highest risk score belongs to the Avenue des Champs-Élysées.

These are largely the roads Paris left out of its 30 km/h limit. When most of the city moved to 30 in August 2021, the Maréchaux, the Champs-Élysées and a short list of other axes kept 50 km/h, among them Boulevard Malesherbes, Cours de Vincennes, Boulevard Diderot and the Voie Georges Pompidou. All of those are in our 20 highest-risk main streets.

At the calm end are main streets with lower speeds and less traffic, such as Rue d'Alésia, Avenue Georges Mandel, Avenue de Saint-Mandé and Boulevard de Grenelle.

These ratings describe how each street is built: its speed limit, traffic, number of lanes, heavy vehicles, and whether the bike lane is a separated track or a line of paint. If you ride one of these streets and think we've got it wrong, write to contact@defensivepedal.com. Corrections go back into the map.

Is the score checked against real crashes?

Yes. We tested it against official crash records from five countries, France among them, covering 2,034 cyclist counting sites, about 4 billion cyclist journeys and 1,169 serious or fatal injuries. On the roads the score rates worst, cyclists were about twice as likely to be seriously injured per journey as on the roads it rates best.

At Paris's own counting sites the rate was 1.9 serious injuries per 10 million cyclist passages. In Bordeaux it was 4.0. The study is public: Severity Without Frequency.

How did Paris get here?

Twenty years ago Paris would have ranked near the bottom. To estimate how far, we took today's map and undid what the city's own records say has changed since 2006. That modelled Paris of 2006 scores about 17 out of 100, roughly 282nd of today's 300 cities. Even the kindest assumptions only lift it to 246th.

The model splits the 77-point climb since then like this:

Change since 2006Index points
30 km/h limit on most streets, since August 2021+42.5
Cycle network: 371 km counted in 2006, 1,607 km at the end of 2025+24.4
Less car traffic (the city counts 60% less than in 2002)+6.9
Car-free riverbanks and pedestrian streets+3.1

The network figures are the city's own counts. Paris changed how it counts cycle routes in 2019, and the new method alone added about a fifth (1,244.5 km instead of 1,037.5 km that year), so the real growth since 2006 is smaller than the two numbers suggest, though still more than threefold.

Lowering speeds did more for the score than building lanes. That matches the crash data, where speed and traffic shape how badly a collision ends. Keep in mind that the 2006 figure is a reconstruction from today's map, not a score anyone measured at the time.

I'm new to cycling. Where do I start in Paris?

Pick the route before the bike. Paris is 6th in Europe for how much of daily life you can reach on connected calm streets, so a calmer way to where you're going usually exists. The work is finding it.

Stay off the Maréchaux and the big squares until you feel at ease. Ride your route once on a weekend, then try it on a workday.

What this score doesn't tell you

The score predicts how badly a crash ends (injury severity), not whether a crash happens.

It was checked against official crash records from major European cities in five countries. That check found the score's signal along streets and not at junctions, where much urban cycling harm happens.

The figures are associations, not causes. Nothing yet shows that changing one rider's route changes their outcome.

A city's score is an average over its whole street network. Risk changes street by street.

Questions about cycling in Paris

Is Paris safer to cycle than Lyon?

The two are too close to separate on this measure, because their likely rank ranges overlap. Paris ranks 13th of 300 with a score of 93.7 (likely 6th–19th), and Lyon ranks 26th with 85.7 (likely 13th–38th). Higher score = lower assessed risk.

What does Paris's score of 93.7 mean?

It places Paris 13th of the 300 European cities in the Pedalability Index 2026. The index runs from 0 to 100 on a fixed scale. Higher score = lower assessed risk. A score of 0.0 means the lowest of the 300 cities, not an absence of safety, and no score means a city is free of risk.

Does the score say how likely a crash is in Paris?

No. The score predicts how badly a crash ends, not whether one happens. It also has no signal at junctions, where much urban cycling harm happens.

Is Paris safer to cycle in than London?

By our measure, yes, by a wide margin. Paris ranks 13th of 300 and London 199th. About 6% of Paris's street length is rated High risk, against 16% of London's.

Why does Paris rank higher than Berlin?

Berlin is 64th, and most of the gap is in journeys and access. Paris is 33rd for a typical cross-town trip and 6th for reaching daily life on calm streets, where Berlin is 119th and 89th. Berlin's streets on their own do better (44th), but its calm streets don't join up as well.

Which French city is best for cycling?

On street risk, Paris (13th), then Lyon (26th), Montpellier (31st), Toulouse (33rd) and Grenoble (35th). Bordeaux is 128th and Marseille 222nd.
